# Product Management Frameworks

## Description
Provides product-management methods used to shape priorities, roadmaps, and outcome tracking. Use these frameworks when the team must decide what to do next and why it matters. The purpose is to connect work to real user outcomes, not to turn everything into a bureaucratic planning exercise.

## Workflow
1. Define the outcome the work is trying to improve.
2. Identify candidate features, fixes, or experiments.
3. Prioritize using the project's value, effort, risk, and dependency context.
4. Convert the chosen work into a roadmap item or actionable scope.
5. Track whether the work moved the outcome that mattered.
6. Keep the product story aligned with requirements and stakeholder expectations.

## Rules
- Tie work to measurable value, not just feature count.
- Use roadmaps to sequence outcomes and dependencies.
- Use OKRs or KPIs only when they clarify the goal and measurement.
- Do not prioritize work without understanding the user and domain impact.
- Keep discovery notes and decisions visible to the delivery team.
- Be clear about what is a commitment, what is a hypothesis, and what is still under discussion.
- Make sure product framing is grounded in actual user needs.

## Project Use
- Use prioritization frameworks (e.g. RICE, MoSCoW, Value vs Effort) when tradeoffs are needed.
- Use outcome targets when the team needs focused direction, not just a task list.
- Keep product framing consistent with documented project goals.
