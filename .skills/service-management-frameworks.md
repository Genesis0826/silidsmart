# Service Management Frameworks

## Description
Provides the service-management framing used when operational structure is needed around incidents, changes, support, and service levels. Use this file when a change affects how the team will run, support, or measure the service. The purpose is to keep service thinking practical: what will break, who responds, how it is restored, and how the team learns from the event.

## Workflow
1. Define the operational concern: incident, problem, change, request, knowledge, or service level.
2. Choose the ITSM practice that fits the situation.
3. Identify the metric or service expectation that will be affected.
4. Write recommendations in a way the team can act on immediately.
5. Capture runbooks, ownership, or follow-up tasks needed to support the service.
6. Record operational guidance in project documentation and runbooks.

## Rules
- Use ITIL 4 practices when they help structure operational response.
- Keep incident, problem, and change concerns distinct.
- Tie service-level discussions to measurable outcomes such as SLA, MTTR, availability, or response time.
- Do not recommend service targets that the current system or team cannot realistically support.
- Make ownership and escalation explicit.
- Keep guidance useful to operators and developers under pressure.
- Make sure operational recommendations scale appropriately with team size and architecture complexity.
